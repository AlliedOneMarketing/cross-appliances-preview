01 — The Read

Who we're actually talking to.

Cross sells appliances that work to people who need them working this week. The customer is often paying with Acima or Snap, is comparing you against a Facebook Marketplace listing, and has been burned before by somebody's cousin selling a dryer out of a garage.

That buyer does not want to be sold. They want proof. Condition, price, and what happens if it breaks — up front, in large type, no asterisk.

The direction, in four rules

  • 01Price is the hero. The number is the largest element on any card, page, or sign. Never smaller than the product name.
  • 02Condition is stated, not implied. Every unit carries a visible grade. Honesty about a dent is what sells the unit next to it.
  • 03Real photos of real units. No manufacturer stock imagery. The customer is buying that machine, serial and all.
  • 04Utility over polish. High contrast, hard edges, no gradients or drop shadows pretending to be depth. It should look like the shop floor, cleaned up.

Feels like

A well-run parts counter. Yellow-and-black machinery labeling. A hardware store price tag. Workwear stitching. Steel shelving under good light.

Does not feel like

A big-box showroom. A luxury kitchen brochure. A startup. Anything with a soft-focus family laughing in a kitchen they clearly do not own.

The one-line test

If a customer standing in the lot on their phone can read the price and condition grade in direct Texas sunlight, it passes. If not, redesign it.

04 — Photography

Shot on the floor, not in a studio.

The photo is the proof. Staff shoot each unit on a phone against a consistent wall, in the same light, from the same angle. Consistency across 200 units beats perfection on three.

✓ DoStraight-on, full unit in frame, even light, plain wall. Same framing every time.
✓ DoA second frame for every flaw. Photograph the dent, the scratch, the missing shelf. Honesty converts.
✗ Don'tDim corners, angled shots, other units in frame, or manufacturer stock photos. Kills trust instantly.

Setup, once

Mark one spot on the floor with tape and hang a neutral backdrop. Every unit gets shot from that spot. Takes 20 seconds per machine.

Minimum set

Three frames per unit: full front, control panel / model plate, and any flaw. Serial photo stays internal for warranty records.

Treatment

No filters. Auto-crop to 1:1 for the grid, 4:3 for detail. The intake tool handles crop and compression automatically.

07 — Voice

Say the number. Say the flaw. Stop talking.

Write it this way

  • "Runs today. 90-day warranty on parts and labor."
  • "Dent on the left side. Doesn't affect operation. That's why it's $299."
  • "Delivered and hooked up in Parker County — Tuesday or Thursday."
  • "No credit? Acima and Snap, approved in the store in about ten minutes."
  • "184 units on the floor right now. This one is the only one at this price."

Never this way

  • "Elevate your home with premium appliance solutions."
  • "Unbeatable prices you won't find anywhere else!!!"
  • "Gently pre-owned, certified quality experience."
  • "Contact us today for a free consultation."
  • "Financing available (subject to approval, see store for details)."

Sentence length

Short. Most lines under twelve words. If a sentence needs a comma to survive, cut it in half.

Numbers over adjectives

"90-day warranty" beats "great warranty." "42 days on floor" beats "priced to move." Specificity is the whole voice.

No exclamation points

Anywhere. The buyer has been shouted at by every used appliance ad in the county. Calm reads as confident.
